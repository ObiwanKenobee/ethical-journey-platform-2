# Atlas — Ethical Supply Chain Intelligence

**Human dignity. Transparency. Accountability. Change.**

Atlas is a human-centered technology initiative focused on making global supply chains more transparent, accountable, and respectful of human rights.

By bringing together artificial intelligence, data intelligence, verification infrastructure, and human expertise, Atlas aims to help organizations identify exploitation risks, improve supplier transparency, strengthen ethical sourcing, and protect vulnerable workers.

> **Our mission:** Build a world where human dignity is protected throughout the supply chains behind the products we make, buy, and use.

## The Problem

Modern slavery, forced labor, human trafficking, and exploitative working conditions remain deeply embedded in global commerce.

Complex international supply chains make it difficult for organizations to establish where products originate, identify vulnerable workers, and verify whether suppliers uphold human rights standards.

Traditional approaches often rely on:

- Periodic supplier audits that provide only limited snapshots of conditions.
- Fragmented supplier records and disconnected compliance systems.
- Manual reporting processes that consume significant organizational resources.
- Limited visibility into subcontractors and suppliers beyond the first tier.
- Inadequate channels for workers to report exploitation safely.

These limitations create opportunities for exploitation to remain hidden.

**Ethical sourcing requires more than periodic compliance checks. It requires continuous visibility, trustworthy evidence, human accountability, and coordinated action.**

## Our Vision

Atlas seeks to become a global intelligence and coordination platform for ethical supply chains.

We envision an ecosystem that connects organizations, suppliers, workers, auditors, civil society, and human rights institutions through trusted information infrastructure.

Rather than treating ethical compliance as a reporting exercise, Atlas aims to make human rights protection an integral part of everyday commercial decision-making.

Our vision is built around four objectives:

1. **Visibility:** Understand the structure and dependencies of global supply networks.
2. **Intelligence:** Identify potential exploitation patterns and emerging risks.
3. **Accountability:** Establish verifiable records and traceable supplier relationships.
4. **Protection:** Connect identified risks with responsible human intervention and support mechanisms.

## Core Capabilities

### 1. AI-Powered Supply Chain Transparency

Apply artificial intelligence and analytical methods to identify patterns that may indicate labor exploitation, unsafe working conditions, or other human rights concerns.

Potential capabilities include:

- Supplier risk profiling.
- Anomaly and pattern detection.
- Risk classification and prioritization.
- Cross-referencing supplier and geographic information.
- Early-warning indicators for emerging risks.
- Explainable assessments that human reviewers can challenge.

**Objective:** Help organizations move beyond fragmented snapshots toward continuous, evidence-informed risk intelligence.

### 2. ESG and Human Rights Compliance

Support organizations in organizing the information needed for ethical sourcing, environmental, social, and governance reporting.

Potential capabilities include:

- Centralized compliance evidence.
- Supplier documentation management.
- Audit and assessment records.
- Regulatory reporting workflows.
- Human rights due diligence documentation.
- Performance and remediation tracking.

The platform is intended to support alignment with relevant frameworks and requirements, including:

- Modern Slavery Act requirements.
- Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD).
- United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).
- Applicable human rights due diligence standards.

Regulatory applicability depends on jurisdiction, organization, and reporting obligations.

### 3. Ethical Sourcing and Supplier Verification

Build a more complete understanding of supplier relationships and the evidence supporting ethical sourcing claims.

Potential capabilities include:

- Supplier registration and assessment.
- Multi-tier supplier mapping.
- Verification of submitted credentials.
- Audit history and evidence management.
- Supplier risk monitoring.
- Corrective action and remediation workflows.

**Objective:** Make supplier transparency an ongoing process rather than a one-time exercise.

### 4. Global Human Rights Risk Intelligence

Combine geographic, supplier, and human rights information to help organizations understand where risks may be concentrated.

Potential capabilities include:

- Geographic risk visualization.
- Regional supply chain intelligence.
- Emerging-risk alerts.
- Human rights and environmental risk overlays.
- Contextual analysis for sourcing decisions.
- Monitoring of changes in identified risk conditions.

### 5. Worker Voice and Protection Networks

Technology should amplify the voices of the people most affected by supply chain decisions.

Atlas envisions secure channels through which workers and trusted organizations can:

- Report concerns and potential violations.
- Access grievance mechanisms.
- Submit contextual information.
- Connect with appropriate support services.
- Track remediation and resolution processes.

Worker safety, informed consent, confidentiality, and protection against retaliation must remain central to the design.

### 6. Enterprise Integration

Atlas aims to connect with existing business infrastructure rather than require organizations to replace every system they already use.

Potential integration areas include:

- Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP).
- Customer Relationship Management (CRM).
- Supplier management platforms.
- Compliance and ESG reporting systems.
- Data warehouses and analytics platforms.
- External verification and intelligence services.

## How Atlas Works

The platform is organized around an evidence-informed decision process:

```text
SUPPLY CHAIN DATA
       |
       v
DATA INGESTION
       |
       v
SUPPLIER MAPPING
       |
       v
RISK INTELLIGENCE
       |
       v
HUMAN REVIEW & VERIFICATION
       |
       v
DECISION & REMEDIATION
       |
       v
MONITORING & REPORTING
       |
       v
CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
```

### From information to action

| Stage | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Observe | Collect relevant supplier, operational, geographic, and human rights information. |
| Map | Establish relationships across supply chain tiers. |
| Analyze | Identify patterns, inconsistencies, and potential risk indicators. |
| Verify | Review findings using evidence, audits, and qualified human expertise. |
| Decide | Prioritize interventions and corrective actions. |
| Act | Coordinate engagement, remediation, and appropriate protection measures. |
| Learn | Monitor outcomes and improve future assessments. |

Atlas is designed to distinguish between reported information, verified evidence, analytical inference, predicted risk, and final decisions.

An AI-generated risk signal should never automatically be treated as proof of exploitation.

## Technology Approach

Atlas explores the combination of several technology disciplines to support trustworthy supply chain intelligence.

| Technology | Intended application |
|---|---|
| Artificial Intelligence | Risk analysis, anomaly detection, pattern recognition, and decision support. |
| Machine Learning | Supplier risk classification and predictive analysis. |
| Data Engineering | Ingestion, transformation, integration, and processing of supply chain information. |
| Geospatial Intelligence | Mapping supplier networks and geographically concentrated risks. |
| Blockchain / Distributed Ledgers | Verifiable records and provenance where decentralized verification offers measurable value. |
| APIs and Integrations | Connecting enterprise systems and external information sources. |
| Data Visualization | Making complex supply networks and risk information understandable. |
| Privacy and Security Engineering | Protecting sensitive organizational and worker information. |

### Architectural Principles

- **Evidence before conclusions:** Every material risk assessment should have an identifiable evidentiary basis.
- **Human oversight:** High-impact decisions require qualified human review.
- **Privacy by design:** Collect only necessary information and protect sensitive identities.
- **Interoperability:** Support integration with existing organizational infrastructure.
- **Explainability:** Make analytical methods, assumptions, and limitations understandable.
- **Scalability:** Design for complex supplier networks and multiple operating environments.
- **Resilience:** Build dependable systems that remain useful across different connectivity and infrastructure conditions.

Blockchain is not assumed to be necessary for every workflow. Its use should be justified by concrete requirements for shared verification, provenance, or tamper-evident records.

## Impact and Accountability

Our objective is not simply to make supply chains more visible. It is to help make them more humane.

Atlas will measure success through outcomes that can be independently assessed.

### Proposed impact indicators

| Area | Measurement |
|---|---|
| Transparency | Percentage of relevant supplier relationships mapped and documented. |
| Risk detection | Verified risk indicators identified and assessed. |
| Response | Time between risk identification and appropriate intervention. |
| Remediation | Percentage of corrective actions completed and independently verified. |
| Worker protection | Safety, accessibility, and resolution outcomes for worker grievance channels. |
| Compliance | Completeness and traceability of required evidence. |
| Enterprise efficiency | Reduction in duplicated reporting and manual evidence-management effort. |
| Long-term impact | Demonstrable improvements in worker protection and labor conditions. |

We will distinguish between platform activity, verified outputs, and real-world human rights outcomes.

**The number of alerts generated is not a measure of lives improved.**

Any public claims about workers protected, countries served, enterprise partners, predictive accuracy, or financial impact should be supported by independently verifiable evidence.

## Guiding Principles

Atlas is built around the conviction that technological progress should serve human dignity.

Our guiding principles are:

- **Human dignity:** People must never be reduced to data points or compliance scores.
- **Transparency:** Make the origin, quality, and limitations of information visible.
- **Accountability:** Ensure that consequential decisions have responsible owners.
- **Justice:** Prioritize the protection of vulnerable people.
- **Integrity:** Never present predictions as verified facts.
- **Collaboration:** Work with institutions, researchers, organizations, suppliers, and workers.
- **Responsible innovation:** Use technology where it creates meaningful and measurable value.

> Their freedom depends on our choices. The products we buy connect us to people we may never meet. Those connections should lead to dignity, not exploitation.

## Security

Supply chain and worker-related information can be highly sensitive.

Security and privacy considerations include:

- Access controls and appropriate authorization.
- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Data minimization and retention policies.
- Secure handling of worker reports and personal information.
- Auditability of sensitive operations.
- Protection against unauthorized disclosure and retaliation.
- Responsible vulnerability reporting.

Do not submit sensitive worker information, confidential supplier records, or personally identifying information through public GitHub issues.

For security concerns, use the project's designated private reporting channel once established.
